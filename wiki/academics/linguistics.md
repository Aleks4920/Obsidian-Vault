# Linguistics

We inherently know sentences have structure. We can question some parts of phrases:
- "Bill is at the cafe" → "Where is Bill?" / "Who is at the cafe?"

## Word Groups

Some words function together in groups. We identify these by replacing groups or moving them around.

- Elon Musk [wants to live on Mars] → Elon Musk [sleeps] ✅
- [Superman said] that he wouldn't buy milk → Can't replace with a verb ❌

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Constituents** | Groups of words that act as one unit |
| **Clauses** | Statements with a subject + predicate |
| **Phrase** | 1+ words acting as a unit |
| **Morphemes** | Minimal meanings that make words |
| **Phones** | Sounds produced |

## Subject of a Sentence

To find the subject: replace the entire subject with a subject pronoun (it, he, she, I, we, they). If it makes sense → that's the subject.

**Example:** "Some old person eating a potato is right here."
- [It] eating a potato is right here. ❌
- Some old person eating a [it] is right here. ❌
- [It] is right here. ✅ — so "Some old person eating a potato" is the subject.

## Predicates of a Sentence

To identify a predicate: replace it with "did so". If it retains meaning with context → it's a predicate.

**Example:** "Some old person eating a potato is right here."
- Some old person [did so] ✅ — "eating a potato is right here" is the predicate.
- Some old [did so] ❌
