# Psychology of Money

**Author:** Morgan Housel  
**Genre:** Finance / Self-Improvement  
**Related:** [[books]], [[finance-tracker]]

---

> "We all think of money from our own experiences but don't actually know how it works"

## Key Ideas

- Personal experience with money shapes our financial beliefs in ways that may not reflect objective reality
- Financial behavior is driven by psychology, not just math — emotions and cognitive biases play a larger role than rational calculation
- Long-term compounding is the most powerful force in wealth building
- Tail events (rare outcomes) drive most returns — most investment gains come from a small number of wins
- Enough is underrated — knowing when you have enough prevents destructive risk-taking
- Wealth is what you don't spend — money not displayed is often more powerful than money displayed
- Time horizon matters enormously — what's rational for a short-term thinker is irrational for a long-term thinker

## Themes

- Humility about financial predictions — everyone has incomplete information
- Saving without a specific goal gives optionality and freedom
- Reasonable > Rational — being approximately right consistently beats being precisely right occasionally
